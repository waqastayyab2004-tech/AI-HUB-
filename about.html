What I actually do

Most of my work sits between two extremes: hand-written scripts that are reliable but rigid, and end-to-end "just ask the model" systems that are flexible but unpredictable. The interesting engineering is in between — designing agents with just enough autonomy to handle real-world variance, and just enough structure to stay debuggable at 2am.

In practice that means: mapping a workflow to an agent graph, deciding what's a tool call versus a model judgment call, building in verification steps where errors are expensive, and instrumenting everything so failures are visible instead of silent.

Why I publish the work

Most "AI agent" content online is either a marketing page or a five-minute demo that skips the part where it breaks. I'd rather show the actual architecture, the actual failure log, and the actual fix — because that's the part that's genuinely useful to someone building their own. Every project on this site shipped in a real workflow before it became a write-up.
